## How much does camping cost in Finland?

__A caravan pitch with electricity for two adults usually costs around 35–45€ per night.__ You can also save some money by staying overnight in your vehicle outside campsites, subject to certain restrictions.

With a tent, it can be easier to find inexpensive or free places to stay in nature. __A tent pitch at a campsite for two adults usually costs around 25–35€ per night.__

For more information about wild camping and staying overnight in your vehicle outside campsites, read the related <Link href="/guides/everymans-rights-and-wild-camping" text="guide"/>.

There is also a middle-ground option for travellers with a campervan, motorhome or caravan. In Finland, these overnight parking areas are often called _matkaparkki_. They usually offer fewer services than a campsite and are therefore cheaper. For example, some ABC caravan parks have offered pitches for around 10€ per night, with electricity available for an additional fee. Prices and services vary between locations, so check the current price before you arrive.

You can also save money with a camping discount card such as Camping Key Europe. In Finland, many participating campsites offer discounts of around 2€ per night, although the exact discount varies by campsite.

<Note text="Many campsites are seasonal and are mainly open from spring to early autumn. If you are travelling outside June–August, check the campsite's opening dates before planning your overnight stop." />

## Is it expensive to drive and park in Finland?

As a 2026 estimate, you can budget approximately:
- __Petrol 95 E10__: around 2.10€ per litre
- __Petrol 98 E5__: around 2.20€ per litre
- __Diesel__: around 2.10€ per litre
- __Biogas (CBG)__: around 2.30€ per kg at Gasum stations
- __Electric vehicle charging__: approximately 0.30–0.80€ per kWh, depending on the charging network and charger

Fuel prices can change significantly, so use these figures as a rough guide rather than fixed prices.

Of course, in Finland, the kilometres can add up, as distances are generally quite long. If you want to pay as little as possible for fuel, you can check local prices using an app called Tankille. It may also be more expensive to fuel up in smaller towns, for example in Lapland, so it may be advisable to fuel up near cities at fuel stations such as ABC or St1.

__Street and city-centre parking fees are around €1–4 per hour__, depending on the city and how close you are to the city centre. In smaller cities, it is easier to find free parking, and there may not be paid parking at all. If your vehicle takes up two spaces, you are better off paying for both spaces, as this is required in some places, although the rules vary from place to place.

__The great thing about driving in Finland is that using the roads is free, as there are no tolls or vignettes needed anywhere.__

## How much does food and drink cost in Finland?

Food is generally somewhat more expensive in Finland than in many other European countries. Your total food budget depends heavily on whether you cook yourself, eat at cafés and fast-food restaurants, or have meals at restaurants.

Here are some rough estimates per adult:
- __Ready-made supermarket meal:__ 3-7€
- __Coffee and pastry at a café:__ 6-10€
- __Lunch buffet:__ 12-15€
- __Fast-food meal:__ 10-15€
- __Casual restaurant meal:__ 15-25€
- __Restaurant dinner:__ 25€ and up
- __Groceries for cooking:__ roughly 10-20€ per person per day

Drinks:
- __Soft drink:__ around 1-2€ from a supermarket vs. €3.50-5.50€ at a restaurant
- __Beer:__ around 1-3€ from a supermarket vs. 6-10€ at a restaurant or bar
- __Wine:__ around 9-18€ per bottle from Alko vs. roughly 30€ and up at a restaurant
- __Drinking water:__ Drinking water is easy to get for free by filling your water bottles from taps, so it doesn't really add to the cost unless you prefer to buy bottled water. A 5-litre container of ordinary bottled water costs around 2.50-3.50€, while a 0.5-litre bottle of premium sparkling water can cost around 3€
