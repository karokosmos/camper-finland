For both international and local campers, it is important to understand what everyman's right allows you to do, especially if you plan to spend time in nature or go wild camping.

## Everyman's rights explained

In Finland, everyman's rights (also known as everyone's rights or the right to roam) give everyone the freedom to enjoy nature, regardless of who owns the land. These rights apply to both Finnish residents and foreign visitors.

Everyman's rights allow you to explore forests, lakes, and other natural areas, as long as you respect nature, other people, and private property. However, these rights also have clear limits. They do not allow you to do anything you want in nature, and some activities require permission or a special permit.

If you are heading to a national park, keep in mind that there may be local rules that restrict everyman's rights, including where camping is allowed. It is a good idea to check the park's rules in advance if you are planning to stay overnight.

## Wild camping with a tent

Since everyman's rights allow you to camp temporarily in many natural areas, wild camping with a tent is easy in Finland. Follow the general rules of everyman's rights, respect nature and other people, and check for local restrictions before pitching your tent, especially in national parks and other protected areas.

## Wild camping with a vehicle

Wild camping is a familiar concept especially for people travelling with campervans or motorhomes. Everyman's rights allow you to stay overnight temporarily (for example, in a tent) in areas where access is otherwise permitted. However, these rights do not apply to motor vehicles. This means that you cannot drive or park a car, caravan, motorhome or campervan off-road in natural areas such as forests or fields, or other natural areas.

But the good news is that Finnish traffic laws generally allow spending the night in a motor vehicle parked beside a road or in a designated parking or rest area, as long as the vehicle is legally parked and does not cause a hazard or obstruct traffic.

In national parks, spending the night inside a camper or motorhome is generally allowed in designated parking areas unless local signs or regulations state otherwise.
